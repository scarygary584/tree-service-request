Hot Springs Storm-Damaged Tree Safety Guide

Use this safety-first checklist after wind, ice, or storm damage. It separates immediate electrical and public-safety concerns from the information a homeowner can gather safely for insurance and a qualified tree professional.

Original homeowner resource

Start with danger—not cleanup

A damaged tree can involve suspended weight, hidden tension, unstable roots, roof damage, blocked access, and energized utility equipment. Do not begin cutting, climbing, pulling, or moving debris merely to inspect the situation.

Five safe decisions

What to do after storm tree damage

1

Check for lines and immediate danger

Assume every low or downed line is energized. Stay away from the line, the tree, water, fencing, vehicles, and anything else that may be touching it. Call 911 for immediate danger and report the line to Entergy.

2

Keep people outside the fall area

Do not stand beneath hanging branches, beside a split trunk, or downhill from an uprooted root plate. Keep children, pets, neighbors, and vehicles away when it can be done safely.

3

Do not climb, cut, or pull

Storm-damaged wood may be suspended or under tension. Do not climb the tree or roof, operate a chainsaw, attach a vehicle or rope, or move debris near utility equipment.

4

Document from a safe location

Record the address, affected structure or access, visible line involvement, and what changed. Take wide-angle photos only from outside the possible fall area. Contact the property insurer when appropriate.

5

Use qualified help for serious damage

The University of Arkansas Extension recommends involving a tree specialist when major branches, bracing, cabling, or serious damage are involved. Confirm qualifications, insurance, scope, cleanup, price, and timing directly.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Should I approach a tree touching a power line?

No. Assume the line is energized, stay away from the tree and anything touching the line, and report it to the utility. Call 911 when there is immediate danger.

Should I walk under a hanging branch to take a photo?

No. Keep out of the possible fall area. Only document damage from a safe location.

Who evaluates a split, leaning, or uprooted tree?

A qualified tree professional should evaluate serious damage. TreeServiceRequest.com does not inspect or diagnose trees.

What should I document for insurance or a provider?

From a safe location, note the address, affected structures or access, visible damage, nearby lines, and wide-angle photos. Do not climb or move debris to get a better picture.

Can I reuse the safety diagram?

Yes, unmodified noncommercial reuse is permitted with visible attribution to TreeServiceRequest.com and a link to this guide.

Does this guide replace emergency, utility, insurance, or professional advice?

No. Follow emergency and utility instructions and obtain advice appropriate to the actual situation.
